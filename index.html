⚛️ Core Concepts

Quantum mechanics is the branch of physics that describes the behavior of matter and energy at the scale of atoms and subatomic particles. Unlike classical physics, it reveals a universe where physical properties occur in discrete packets called quanta — hence the name. It is the theoretical foundation behind technologies like quantum computing, transistors, lasers, and MRI machines. The framework of quantum theory rests on several core, interconnected ideas that challenge everything we intuitively know about reality: Superposition, Wave-particle duality, Quantum entanglement, and Heisenberg's uncertainty principle.

📝 Core Contents

  • Superposition
    One of the most fascinating principle of quantum mechanics is superposition—the ability of a quantum system to be in multiple states at the same time until it is measured. This principle is famously illustrated by Schrödinger's cat paradox.
  • Wave particle duality
    • The double slit experiment was one of the greatest breakthroughs in the history of science. The experiment shows both particle and wave nature of quantum objects. Renowned physicist Richard Feynman famously called it as "a phenomenon which is impossible to explain in any classical way".
  • Quantum Entanglement
    • Quantum entanglement is a phenomenon where particles become interconnected such that the state of one particle instantly influences the state of another, regardless of the distance between them. Einstein famously called this "spooky action at a distance."
  • Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle
    • Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle states that it is impossible to know both the exact position and the exact momentum of an object simultaneously. The more precisely one is known, the less precisely the other can be determined
